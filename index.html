Welcome to MooSynth

Target audience: food science and dairy technology students, dairy-plant trainees and culinary students who need to understand where milk comes from before they process it.

Interactive Notes: Milk Biosynthesis

Lactation and the milking cycle

  • Milk is the source of nutrients and immune protection for the calf. Gestation in a cow is 9 months.
  • At parturition the udder secretes colostrum: yellowish, salty, very high in serum protein, rich in antibodies. It returns to normal milk after 72 hours.
  • The lactation period is about 305 days and yields 9,000 to 10,000 kg or more, while a calf needs only about 1,000 kg.
  • Peak yield is 2 to 3 months after calving (40 to 50 L/day). A cow can have 5 to 6 cycles, with a peak in the 3rd.

Stage 1: Digestion

Milk components come mostly from digested food. Enzymes break food into simple, soluble pieces:

  • These nutrients, plus salts, glycerol and organic acids, are carried by the blood to all organs, including the mammary gland (the udder).
  • They provide energy and precursors for making milk proteins and lipids.
💡 It takes 400 to 800 L of blood to deliver the components for 1 L of milk.

Stage 2: Synthesis in the alveolus

  • The alveolus is the milk-producing unit, lined with a single layer of epithelial secretory cells, surrounded by myoepithelial cells and blood capillaries.
  • Inside the cell, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and its ribosomes make milk components. Mitochondria supply the energy.
  • The Golgi apparatus packs the components into vesicles.
  • Vesicles cross the cytoplasm and the apical plasma membrane and are released into the lumen, the central storage area.
  • The lumen connects through ducts to the cistern.

Stage 3: Excretion (milk let-down)

  • Milk does not leave the udder spontaneously. The alveoli must contract through the muscle tissue (myoepithelial cells) around them.
  • Contraction is triggered by the hormone oxytocin, released into the blood when the teats are stimulated by the suckling calf or the milker.
  • Milk flows from the lumen through small ducts into larger ones to the cistern, then out through the teat.
  • A cow has four teats and four separate mammary glands called udder quarters.
💡 Milk is “filtered blood”: the udder builds milk from blood nutrients.

  • Solution and dispersion: fat globules (with a protective membrane) and casein micelles are dispersed in water. Creaming by gravity or centrifugation gives cream and skim milk.
  • Nutrition: a near-complete food but poor in iron and not high in vitamin C, and it lacks fibre.
  • Bacteria: nutrient-rich, so many bacteria grow in milk, especially at high temperatures.

Common mistakes & troubleshooting

  • No camera prompt appears: the page must be served over https. Open the app from its web address, not from a downloaded file inside an email or chat.
  • Permission was blocked: click the padlock or camera icon in the address bar, set Camera to Allow, then reload.
  • Inside Google Sites the camera does not start: use the “Open full screen” link on the landing page, which opens the app in its own tab where permissions work.
  • Count keeps jumping: improve lighting (face a window or lamp), use a plain background, keep the hand still and fully in frame.
  • Wrong count with the thumb: for 4, fold the thumb across your palm. For 1, curl the other fingers firmly.
  • Camera used by another app: close Zoom, Teams or other tabs using the camera.
  • Slow or laggy: close other tabs; use Chrome or Edge on a laptop.
  • Backup mode: if gestures fail (for example during a demo), press keys 1 to 4 or click an option.
